<h2>Business challenge</h2>
<p>The business lacked a strong pay structure, allowing managers to set salaries with minimal oversight, which left it vulnerable to equal pay claims. Efforts to address this were hampered by resistance from long-serving union members benefiting from annual percentage increases, worsening the disparity over time. Consequently, progress in tackling pay disparity had been minimal.</p>
<h2>Our collaboration</h2>
We collaborated with the CEO and Executive Team to establish principles for a long-term pay strategy. After agreement, we engaged senior managers to model the impact of proposed changes and identify implementation challenges. Aware of pay sensitivities, we communicated early with unions and employees to explain our intentions. We believed this would improve ballot success. Union representatives joined senior manager meetings during scoping, and employees attended briefings with tools to understand individual impacts.
<p>What we learned:</p><p>Spending time on early and ongoing engagement is vital to ensure people feel heard.<br/>It’s important to agree on the scope and non-negotiables with the business from the start and understand the impact of these decisions, especially when there are significant financial implications.</p>
<h2>Benefits</h2>
<ul><li>Implementing a robust pay structure has greatly reduced the risk of future equal pay claims.</li><li>This framework ensures fairness and consistency in employee compensation and provides managers with clear guidance for their decisions.</li></ul>
